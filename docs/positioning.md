# Edge Solutions Positioning

This file owns the market promise, target audience, offer hierarchy, and tone for `edge-solutions.be`.

## Positioning Statement

Edge Solutions presents Gil as an experienced pragmatic builder: senior in realtime 3D / XR applications and technical delivery, experienced in multidisciplinary technical production, hands-on in LLM-based AI automation, and focused on turning technical ideas into usable project outcomes.

The public offer is focused on two clear project families: AI automation and 3D / XR applications. It remains AI-first for acquisition priority, but the homepage presents both families with equal visual weight so the 3D / XR offer does not feel artificially minor. It is not a generic agency, flat services menu, recruiter profile, or SaaS product.

## Target Audience

- Belgium-first teams, especially Wallonia-facing teams.
- SMEs, startups, agencies, and technical decision-makers.
- Teams feeling friction around AI adoption, automation, data/process handoffs, interactive 3D / XR applications, or unclear technical delivery.

## Offer Hierarchy

1. Primary promise: practical AI automation that starts by auditing repeated tasks, finding where lightweight intelligence can help, and making work easier for people inside the company.
2. Supporting specialist depth: realtime 3D and XR applications, including VR, augmented reality, configurators, simulations, training experiences, performance, and technical recovery.
3. Tone: concrete, calm, technical, no hype, no black box, no fake authority signals. Use "senior" for Gil's 3D / XR, realtime, and broader technical delivery experience; do not imply senior-level depth in AI/LLM work.

## Copywriting Guardrails

The website should read like a senior freelance developer explaining clearly how he can help, not like a SaaS product, agency pitch, or third-party bio.

- Write directly and simply. A visitor should understand quickly who Gil is, what he can help with, and how to start a conversation.
- Do not use third-person or editorial framing such as "Qui intervient", "ce freelance", or copy that sounds like someone else is presenting Gil.
- Avoid repeating the same identity claims on one page. State who Gil is once, then use later sections to explain help, process, work examples, and next steps.
- Do not apologize for missing client case studies or public client references. Avoid phrases such as "en attendant des cas clients publiables". Present available public material confidently as concrete elements the visitor can evaluate.
- Avoid vague credibility language such as "operational validation", "grandes études de cas publiques", or "promesse commerciale" unless it is tied to a visible source, tool, or artifact.
- Avoid obvious or generic claims as section titles, such as "clarifier ce qu'il faut construire" or "développer ce qui sert l'usage", unless the section names a concrete action, deliverable, or decision.
- Process copy should describe how a mission works: context, scope, short deliveries, usable version, stabilization, documentation, transfer, or next iteration. It should not pretend all projects follow one rigid method.
- Do not merge AI automation and 3D / XR into a single undifferentiated promise. Shared process sections can cover both, but service copy should keep their project types, inputs, and outputs distinct.
- For public copy, never use an em dash (—). Use a short sentence, comma, colon, or simple hyphen instead.
- Avoid taxonomy stacking such as "3D, VR & XR" or "VR et AR/XR". XR already covers VR and AR, so use clean titles like "Applications 3D temps réel et XR" / "Real-time 3D and XR applications". Mention "VR" and "réalité augmentée" / "augmented reality" naturally in body copy, metadata, alt text, or project descriptions when those terms help users and search engines understand the concrete scope.
- Prefer client-language French over internal delivery jargon. Use "processus", "mise en production", "livraison", "première version", "documentation", and "transfert" when they are clearer than "workflow", "prod", or "delivery".
- Keep claims grounded in real scope. AI/LLM work can be described as hands-on or experienced; "senior" belongs to realtime 3D / XR and broader technical delivery experience.
- Use Gil's lead and production background to support project clarity, maintainable architecture, coordination, and pragmatic delivery. Do not turn the website into an engineering-manager or recruiter-facing profile.
- AI-assisted development can mention the real tool stack Gil is currently exploring or using, including OpenClaw, CrewAI, OpenCode, Codex, and Cursor since late 2024. Present this as an evolving working stack, not as a maturity or certification claim.
- If a section or visual block repeats a point already made nearby, remove or simplify it. The site should feel direct and approachable, not padded.

## AI Automation Approach

When describing AI automation, frame the work around people and useful repetition:

- Start by understanding how work is done today, then identify repeated tasks, document handling, qualification, summaries, checks, or handoffs that consume attention.
- Use automation when rules are enough, and add LLM-based intelligence only where interpretation, language, prioritization, or context handling is genuinely useful.
- The goal is not to replace people or add AI everywhere. The goal is to make work easier so people in the company can focus on judgment, relationships, craft, and the work they do best.
- Keep human review and action limits where the task is sensitive, ambiguous, or consequential.

## Anti-Drift Rules

- Do not flatten the whole site into a generic services menu. Keep AI first in order and acquisition priority, but use equal visual weight for the two homepage service cards.
- Do not optimize the site for recruiter-facing positioning.
- Do not invent polished case studies when truthful process artifacts are stronger.
- Do not reduce the 3D / XR offer to prototypes only; prototypes are one stage, but the public offer includes interactive applications, experiences, and production-minded realtime work.
- Do not add decorative complexity to compensate for unclear copy. Fix the message first.
- Judge design and content recommendations against this positioning before generic agency-site heuristics.
