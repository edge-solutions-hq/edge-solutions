# Content Plan for Multilingual Freelance Portfolio Website

## Overview and Structure

- **Single-Page Layout with Clear Sections:** The site will use a one-page design divided into distinct sections for each service, ensuring easy navigation without overwhelming the user. A concise top navigation menu will allow jumping to the **Web Development** section or the **Real-Time Technology** section directly[\[1\]](https://www.format.com/magazine/resources/photography/multiple-photography-niches#:~:text=Instead%2C%20opt%20for%20a%20template,work%20relevant%20to%20that%20niche). This approach respects the preference for a single-page layout while keeping content organized for clarity.
- **Distinct Service Separation:** The two offerings (Web Development and Real-Time 3D/VR Technology) are presented in separate sections with their own headings and content. This prevents any confusion about what service is being described at any given time, as each section is clearly labeled and self-contained[\[1\]](https://www.format.com/magazine/resources/photography/multiple-photography-niches#:~:text=Instead%2C%20opt%20for%20a%20template,work%20relevant%20to%20that%20niche). Visitors interested in one specialty can focus on that section without wading through the other.
- **Key Components Included:** The plan incorporates all essential elements of a strong freelance portfolio: a compelling **hero intro**, a brief **about** segment, clearly defined **services** sections for each offering, a showcase of **portfolio/projects**, and prominent **call-to-action** (CTA) and **contact** options. This aligns with best practices for portfolio sites: highlighting your work, explaining your background, showcasing skills, and making it easy to get in touch[\[2\]](https://www.wix.com/blog/best-portfolio-websites#:~:text=,unique%20approaches%20or%20real%20impact)[\[3\]](https://www.wix.com/blog/best-portfolio-websites#:~:text=Make%20getting%20in%20touch%20simple,that%20proves%20value%20and%20makes).
- **Primary Language & Translations:** The default content will be in **French**, with the structure set up to accommodate English and Dutch translations for each section in the future. All text components (headings, descriptions, buttons, form labels) will be translated to maintain a consistent user experience across languages. The content plan ensures each section remains in the same order and format across languages for familiarity.
- **Navigation & Language Toggle:** A persistent header will contain the site logo/name, navigation links, and a language switcher. The language toggle will be **highly visible in the top-right** of the menu (a common placement), so users can easily switch to their preferred language[\[4\]](https://www.weglot.com/blog/website-language-selector#:~:text=Make%20It%20Easy%20to%20Find). Since three languages are planned, a dropdown selector listing **FR / EN / NL** is recommended for a clean look[\[5\]](https://www.weglot.com/blog/website-language-selector#:~:text=Consider%20How%20Many%20Language%20Versions,You%20Plan%20to%20Offer). This way, non-French speakers immediately see the option to switch, rather than assuming the site isn't in their language and leaving[\[4\]](https://www.weglot.com/blog/website-language-selector#:~:text=Make%20It%20Easy%20to%20Find).

## Header & Navigation (with Language Switch)

- **Purpose:** Provide a clear global navigation and language selection tool accessible from any part of the page. The header ensures visitors can instantly navigate to the section that interests them (Web Development or Real-Time Tech) and switch languages if needed.
- **Content & Elements:** The header will be a simple top bar containing:
- **Logo/Name:** The freelancer's name or logo on the left for branding and a professional touch.
- **Menu Links:** Two primary navigation links - e.g., "Web Development" and "Real-Time 3D/VR" - plus possibly "Portfolio" and "Contact" anchors. These links scroll to their respective sections on the single page. Having a **large, legible menu with a clear item for each service niche** is crucial so each audience can jump directly to what they need[\[1\]](https://www.format.com/magazine/resources/photography/multiple-photography-niches#:~:text=Instead%2C%20opt%20for%20a%20template,work%20relevant%20to%20that%20niche). For example, a business client looking for web development can click "Web Development" immediately without scrolling through unrelated content.
- **Language Switcher:** A language selector on the right, prominently displayed (using text labels like FR/EN/NL or a globe icon with dropdown). This will allow instant switching between French, English, and Dutch. It should be easy to find - placing it in the header or navigation menu is a best practice, as users typically look to the top of the page for language options[\[4\]](https://www.weglot.com/blog/website-language-selector#:~:text=Make%20It%20Easy%20to%20Find). With three languages, a dropdown menu is preferred for a cleaner interface[\[5\]](https://www.weglot.com/blog/website-language-selector#:~:text=Consider%20How%20Many%20Language%20Versions,You%20Plan%20to%20Offer).
- **Key Message:** While the header itself is more functional than narrative, it reinforces professionalism and user-friendliness. The presence of the language switch and clear menu demonstrates that the site caters to an international audience and has well-organized content.
- **Relationship to User Goals:** This component directly addresses the goal of keeping the two services distinct and accessible. The dedicated menu items for each service prevent any confusion by letting users filter content from the start[\[1\]](https://www.format.com/magazine/resources/photography/multiple-photography-niches#:~:text=Instead%2C%20opt%20for%20a%20template,work%20relevant%20to%20that%20niche). Likewise, the language switch supports the multilingual goal, ensuring French speakers see content in French by default, but others can easily navigate to English or Dutch. By making these options obvious, the site avoids frustrating or losing potential clients due to language barriers or navigation confusion.

## Hero Section (Introduction)

- **Purpose:** Form a strong first impression and immediately communicate who the freelancer is and the two main services offered. The hero section grabs the visitor's attention and provides a quick overview so they instantly understand the website's offering.
- **Key Message:** A concise tagline will convey the dual expertise. For example: **"Freelance Web Developer & Real‑Time 3D Specialist"**. This one-liner (in French by default, e.g., "Développeur Web & Spécialiste 3D Temps Réel") instantly tells visitors that the freelancer works in both web development and real-time/VR domains. It sets a professional and confident tone, showing a unique blend of skills.
- **Content & Elements:**
- **Headline/Name:** The hero can include the freelancer's name (e.g., _"Gil Guminski"_) with a title or short descriptor.
- **Tagline:** As mentioned, a brief tagline highlighting the two service areas. This could be one sentence or a few key phrases (possibly stacked or separated by typography) that include keywords like _"PHP/Laravel & Web Apps"_ and _"Unity/Unreal & VR Experiences"_ to cover both domains.
- **Visual Background:** An eye-catching hero image or graphic that subtly combines elements of both web and 3D tech. For instance, a faint collage of code or a website layout on one side and a 3D scene or VR headset on the other. This visually reinforces the two specializations. (The image should be professional and not too cluttered, possibly abstract, to avoid favoring one service over the other.)
- **Primary CTA Button:** A clear call-to-action in the hero, such as a button labeled "**Contact Me**" or "**Discuss Your Project**" (in French: "_Contactez-moi_"), encouraging immediate action. Alternatively, it could say "Explore Web Development" and "Explore Real-Time 3D" as two separate buttons if the user wants to guide visitors directly to one of the two sections. However, one primary CTA (e.g., contact) is usually effective to start the conversion funnel.
- **Language Indicator (if not already obvious):** If the site is primarily in French, the hero might subtly indicate this (like a small French flag or text in the corner) in addition to the header switcher, just to reassure French-speaking visitors that they're seeing the French content. This is optional since the language switcher in the header may suffice.
- **Relationship to User Goals:** The hero section contributes to **clearly separating the two offerings** right from the top by naming both, thus immediately addressing the concern of confusion. A visitor doesn't have to guess what the freelancer does - it's stated upfront. By projecting a polished tagline and design, it also **presents the services professionally**, appealing to business clients and agencies who form the target audience. Finally, the inclusion of a CTA aims to start driving freelance inquiries (the ultimate goal) by prompting contact or exploration as soon as interest is piqued.

## About Me Section (Brief Bio)

- **Purpose:** Build trust and provide context about the freelancer's background. Business clients and agencies will want to know who the person is, their experience level, and why they are capable in these two domains. The About section personalizes the site and adds credibility.
- **Key Message:** A short overview of the freelancer's **experience and values**. For example: "**Tech Lead with 10+ years experience in web and game development**. Passionate about solving problems and creating immersive experiences." This message should highlight the unique combination of skills (web + real-time tech) and possibly the professional ethos (reliability, innovation, etc.). It establishes the freelancer as an expert in both fields, not a dabbler, by mentioning relevant roles or achievements (e.g., _former Technical Director in VR startup, experienced in managing web projects_).
- **Content & Elements:**
- **Brief Bio Text:** 1-2 short paragraphs (3-5 sentences each) introducing the person. This will be in French primarily (with equivalent translations ready for other languages). It can mention location (e.g., _Belgium-based_), years of experience, and a sentence on the approach to work (for instance, emphasizing problem-solving and continuous learning, as hinted in the CV). It should also mention the dual expertise in a cohesive way. For example: "_After a decade in tech, I've honed my skills in both building_ _web platforms_ _and crafting_ _real-time 3D applications_\*\*..." - showing the two niches as part of one narrative. According to multi-niche portfolio advice, if there's a story connecting the two specialties, it's good to briefly share it[\[6\]](https://www.format.com/magazine/resources/photography/multiple-photography-niches#:~:text=If%20there%E2%80%99s%20a%20good%20reason,you%20are%20in%20your%20career). In this case, the narrative could be that working on interactive 3D projects (games, VR) and web development are both about creating engaging user experiences, just in different mediums - demonstrating a broad passion for development that spans multiple platforms.
- **Profile Photo:** A professional-looking photo of the freelancer (optional but recommended for personal connection). This could be placed alongside the text. It humanizes the site, which is important for freelancers.
- **Key Achievements Highlights:** Possibly a few bullet points or a small timeline of major roles/projects, if space allows. For example, "Tech Lead at XYZ Company (2025)", "Co-founder of VR startup (2023)", "15+ web projects delivered". Keep it very brief or even integrated into the paragraph, to avoid a long resume on the page. The full details can be on LinkedIn or a downloadable CV.
- **Link to Resume/LinkedIn:** A small call-out like "**View full CV**" or LinkedIn icon can be included for those who want to dig deeper. This keeps the on-page content short (supporting the goal of brevity) while still offering depth for interested clients. This content can be hidden behind a link to avoid clutter: for instance, a **"Read more about my experience"** toggle that expands to show a few more details or a PDF link.
- **Relationship to User Goals:** This section **supports professional presentation** by showcasing the freelancer's credibility (experience and expertise). It reassures business clients and startups that the person is not only skilled but also has relevant background to deliver results. By briefly explaining the dual focus, it also helps prevent confusion - visitors understand _why_ both services are offered and that the freelancer is qualified in both (as recommended, explaining multiple niches can make them feel more natural and connected[\[6\]](https://www.format.com/magazine/resources/photography/multiple-photography-niches#:~:text=If%20there%E2%80%99s%20a%20good%20reason,you%20are%20in%20your%20career)). Keeping it concise or behind a toggle means the page remains clean and focused (important for busy clients), but the information is there for those who seek it.

## Web Development Services Section

- **Purpose:** Present the **Web Development** service offering in depth, targeting clients interested in websites or web applications. This section functions as a mini "service page" for web dev, outlining what is offered and why the freelancer is a great choice for web projects.
- **Key Message:** The freelancer delivers **modern, robust web solutions** using industry-standard technologies. A potential headline for this section could be, for example: _"Custom Web Development - Scalable, Secure, and Tailored to Your Business."_ The messaging should emphasize benefits (e.g., scalable architecture, user-friendly design, performance, security) and the freelancer's proficiency in relevant frameworks. It establishes that for any web-based project (be it a corporate website, e-commerce, or a web app), the freelancer has the skills (PHP, Laravel, React, etc.) to execute it professionally.
- **Content & Elements:**
- **Section Title:** A clear heading like "**Web Development**" (and in French: "_Développement Web_") at the start of this section, possibly with a brief tagline. For instance, under the title: "_Building fast and reliable websites & applications_".
- **Service Description:** A concise paragraph outlining what is offered in web development. This might include: _development of custom websites and web applications, front-end and back-end integration, content management solutions (CMS), e-commerce development, and ongoing maintenance_. It should be written in a business-friendly tone, focusing on outcomes (e.g., _"I create websites that are not only visually appealing but also scalable and secure, ensuring your online presence grows with your business."_). Technical terms like "Laravel" or "React" can be mentioned, but balanced with layman benefits (so a client understands the value).
- **Key Technologies List:** Since specific technologies are listed (PHP, Laravel, WordPress, Next.js, React, Vue.js), they should appear here, but in a clean format. This could be a short bulleted list or a series of icons/logos of each tech, showing the tech stack proficiency. To avoid overwhelming non-technical clients, this list can be visually de-emphasized or put under a collapsible "Tech stack" section. For example, show the primary ones up front (Laravel, React, WordPress) and hide the rest under a "+ More" toggle. This way, tech-savvy visitors or agency folks can expand to see all capabilities, while others aren't swamped with jargon by default.
- **Specific Services Bullet Points:** It may help to list specific types of web projects handled. For instance:
  - _Corporate Websites & Blogs (WordPress integration for easy content management)_
  - _Single Page Applications (using React or Vue for dynamic user experience)_
  - _E-commerce Platforms (secure online stores with payment integration)_
  - _Web APIs and Backends (Laravel PHP backends, MySQL databases, GraphQL APIs)_ These points communicate the range within web development, and each can be kept brief. If space is an issue, these can be combined into a sentence format rather than a list.
- **Portfolio Example (Web):** Integrate a highlight from a web project. For example, show a screenshot or name-drop a successful project ("Developed the website for **Client X**, resulting in 50% traffic increase"). This could be a small image with a caption or a short case study blurb. Only one or two examples should be here, as a teaser - the full portfolio section later will have more. If details are lengthy, link to a case study page or use a "read more" expansion.
- **Call-to-Action:** End this section with a focused CTA for web clients. For instance, a line of text and button: _"Have a web project in mind?_ _Let's Talk."_ This button would jump to the contact section or open a contact form, pre-filling the subject as a web inquiry. By placing a CTA here, a business client sold on web services doesn't have to hunt for how to contact.
- **Relationship to User Goals:** This Web Development section keeps content **highly relevant to a target subset of the audience** (businesses or agencies needing web work) and clearly separate from the real-time/VR content. By structuring it with its own heading and list of services, a visitor interested in web development will immediately recognize they are in the right place, satisfying the goal of avoiding confusion. The professional tone and inclusion of tech keywords cater to agencies/tech-savvy clients, while the emphasis on business outcomes (like scalable, secure, user-friendly) appeals to business owners - together, this **professional presentation of services** builds confidence. The strategically placed CTA supports the goal of driving inquiries, as it gently guides a ready prospect toward making contact after seeing what they need.

## Real-Time Technology Services Section

- **Purpose:** Highlight the **Real-Time Technology** services (such as game development, VR/AR experiences, 3D graphics, and interactive simulations) in a dedicated section. This targets clients interested in cutting-edge interactive experiences or 3D projects, ensuring they see all relevant info in one place distinct from web services.
- **Key Message:** The freelancer creates **immersive and interactive experiences** using advanced real-time 3D technologies. A possible headline could be: _"Real‑Time 3D & VR Development - Interactive Solutions for Modern Problems."_ The messaging should stress capabilities like creating virtual environments, simulations, or games that deliver engagement and innovation. It positions the freelancer as an expert in engines like Unreal and Unity, able to bring ideas to life in real-time graphics.
- **Content & Elements:**
- **Section Title:** For example, "**Real-Time 3D & VR**" (in French "_Technologies Temps Réel & VR_"), clearly labeling this section. A subheading might add: "_Unreal Engine, Unity, WebGL - High-End Interactive Development_".
- **Service Description:** A short paragraph describing what this service entails. For example: _"I develop real-time applications including virtual reality experiences, 3D simulations, and interactive visuals. Whether it's a VR training program, a WebGL product demo, or a gaming prototype, I leverage game engines to create smooth, responsive and immersive user experiences."_ This explanation tells a non-expert client what they can get (VR apps, simulations, etc.) while also nodding to the tech for those in the know.
- **Key Technologies & Skills:** List the core tools: Unreal Engine 4/5, Unity 3D, WebGL (Three.js or Babylon.js could be mentioned if relevant), VR hardware (like Oculus/Meta VR, HTC Vive) and possibly AR if applicable. Similar to the web section, present these as icons or a brief list. If additional details exist (like experience with AWS for game servers, C++/C# for game scripting, etc.), these can be mentioned in an expanded blurb or hidden under a "More technical details" toggle to keep the initial view client-friendly.
- **Specific Offerings Bullet Points:** Outline the types of projects in this realm:
  - _Virtual Reality Applications (e.g. VR training simulations, VR games)_
  - _Augmented/Mixed Reality Experiences (if offered, e.g. AR mobile apps)_
  - _3D Web Experiences (WebGL-based interactive graphics on websites)_
  - _Game Development and Prototyping (game mechanics, interactive demos)_
  - _Real-Time Visualization (architectural walkthroughs, product visualizers in real-time)_ Each bullet gives an idea of use-cases. This list assures potential clients (like tech startups or agencies) that the freelancer can handle their particular type of project.
- **Portfolio Example (Real-Time):** Include a highlight of a real-time tech project. For instance, _"Lead developer for a VR multiplayer game"_ or _"Created a WebGL 3D product showcase for_ _Client Y"_. A small image (like a screenshot from the Unreal Engine project or a VR app snapshot) plus a one-sentence caption about the outcome (e.g., _"Built a VR training simulation for a medical company to help train surgeons remotely"_). As with the web section, keep details brief and link out or expand for more info if necessary.
- **Call-to-Action:** A tailored CTA such as _"Interested in AR/VR or 3D development?_ _Contact me to discuss."_ placed at the end of this section. This ensures that someone excited about the real-time services has an immediate next step (jumping to the contact form) just as the web-focused visitor did in the previous section.
- **Relationship to User Goals:** This section ensures the **Real-Time Tech services are equally prominent and distinct**. By separating it from the web content, any visitor with interest in interactive 3D or VR will directly find what they need, achieving the goal of clarity between offerings. The professional tone and examples here speak to agencies or startups in the gaming/VR/tech space, showing that the freelancer understands that domain deeply (which is crucial for **professional presentation** to that audience). Including a few concrete examples or project types helps business clients envision how these services could apply to them (reducing any ambiguity about what "real-time tech" means). The CTA again drives the conversion goal, inviting specialized inquiries. Overall, this section stands as a parallel to the web section - equal in depth and clarity - so both service areas are well-represented on the site.

## Portfolio Section (Selected Projects)

- **Purpose:** Showcase **selected projects** from both service categories to provide evidence of quality and experience. This section builds credibility by showing real-world applications of the freelancer's skills and the results achieved, thereby convincing potential clients of the value offered.
- **Key Message:** _"Proven Results in Web & 3D Projects"_ - the portfolio communicates that the freelancer has successfully delivered projects in both domains. The key message is that the work is **high-quality and relevant**, whether it's a complex website or a cutting-edge VR app. It should implicitly tell clients: "I've done something similar to what you need, and I can do it well."
- **Content & Elements:**
- **Section Layout:** The portfolio will be organized in a way that keeps **web and real-time projects distinguishable**. One approach is to divide the section into two parts:
  - **Web Projects:** A subheading or column for web development case studies.
  - **Real-Time Projects:** A subheading or column for real-time/VR case studies. This could be a two-column layout on desktop (one column per category) and stacked on mobile. Alternatively, a filter toggle (buttons labeled "Web" and "Real-Time") can allow users to switch the view between the two sets, showing one category at a time - but it might be simpler to just display both with labels.
- **Project Entries:** For each featured project, include:
  - A **thumbnail image** or screenshot (e.g., a homepage of a website for a web project, a snapshot of a 3D scene for a VR project).
  - **Project name or client name** (if not confidential) and perhaps the year.
  - A **one-liner description** highlighting what it was and a key outcome. For example: _"E-commerce Website for_ _RetailClient_ _- Developed a scalable online store with Laravel, resulting in a 35% increase in sales"_ or _"VR Training App for_ _MedicalCo_ _- Built in Unreal Engine to simulate surgical procedures for remote learning."_ These descriptions focus on the benefit or result (whenever possible quantify or qualify success), which is persuasive for business readers.
  - Possibly **tags or labels** indicating the technologies used (for those who care, e.g., "Laravel, MySQL" or "Unity, Oculus VR"), but these should be subtle (could be shown on hover or in a smaller font) so as not to clutter the main message.
  - If more detail is available, a **"Read more" link** or a clickable card can open a dedicated project detail page or a modal with more screenshots and a fuller case study (in multiple languages as well). However, in a one-page site, it might be preferable to link to an external page or PDF for case studies to keep the one-page from becoming too long.
- **Number of Projects:** Aim to showcase perhaps **3-4 projects per category** (depending on how many strong examples are available). Too few and the site might look thin; too many and it becomes overwhelming. If the freelancer has many projects, pick the most impressive and recent ones for 2026 relevance. If needed, provide a _"View all projects"_ link that could lead to a portfolio page or simply note that more can be provided on request, to keep the page concise.
- **Visual Consistency:** Use a uniform style for project thumbnails (all screenshots in the same size or aspect ratio) to make the portfolio look neat. Under each subheading (Web, Real-Time), the entries should be consistent in format.
- **Optional - Testimonials:** If any client testimonials or brief quotes are available, they could be strategically placed in this section (or in a sidebar) to add trustworthiness. For instance, a quote from a client of a web project praising reliability, and one from a VR project client praising innovation. This isn't a required component, but it would **add value and credibility** if available (as portfolio best practices suggest including testimonials or case studies can bolster trust[\[3\]](https://www.wix.com/blog/best-portfolio-websites#:~:text=Make%20getting%20in%20touch%20simple,that%20proves%20value%20and%20makes)).
- **Relationship to User Goals:** The portfolio section directly underpins the goal of **presenting services professionally** - by backing up claims with real examples. It helps convert interested visitors into actual inquiries because seeing tangible work reduces uncertainty. By separating the projects into the two categories, it again **keeps the services distinct**: a client interested in web work can look only at the web portfolio pieces, and a VR client can focus on the real-time projects. This organization shows respect for the visitor's time and needs, which is a professional touch. Moreover, showing strong projects for each target audience (e.g., a startup website for web, a VR demo for a tech company) can appeal to those segments (agencies, businesses, startups) by reflecting their own project types. Keeping descriptions succinct with the option to read more ensures that the content is **comprehensive but not overwhelming** - detailed information is available for those who want it, but by default only the key points (what the project was, and what was achieved) are visible. This aligns with the idea of using toggles or "read more" to maintain a clean look while still offering depth when needed.

## Call-to-Action & Contact Section

- **Purpose:** Drive the visitor to take the final step and initiate contact. This section is the conversion point - after a visitor has learned about the services and seen the portfolio, it should be very easy for them to reach out for an inquiry or quote. It's both a CTA and a Contact section, typically the last part of the page.
- **Key Message:** A friendly yet professional invitation for the viewer to get in touch. For example: _"Ready to start your project? Let's work together."_ (French: "_Prêt à donner vie à votre projet ? Discutons-en._") - a phrase that encourages action and positions the collaboration as a partnership. The tone should be encouraging and confident, making the visitor feel their inquiry is welcome.
- **Content & Elements:**
- **CTA Heading/Text:** One or two sentences reinforcing the eagerness to help and the range of expertise. E.g., _"Whether you need a new website or an immersive VR experience, I'm here to help._ _Contact me_ _to discuss your ideas and get a free consultation."_ This reiterates the two service domains to remind them they can ask about either. Mention of a "_free consultation_" or "_free quote_" is a common strategy to encourage contact by reducing hesitation (and can be offered if the user is open to it)[\[3\]](https://www.wix.com/blog/best-portfolio-websites#:~:text=Make%20getting%20in%20touch%20simple,that%20proves%20value%20and%20makes).
- **Contact Form:** A simple form with 3-5 fields maximum: Name, Email, Message, and perhaps a dropdown or checkboxes for type of project (Web or Real-Time or "Not sure/Other") - the latter could help gather what they're interested in, but even without it, the message field can capture their needs. Keep the form minimal to not intimidate; according to UX best practices, forms that don't overwhelm users encourage them to complete the contact process[\[3\]](https://www.wix.com/blog/best-portfolio-websites#:~:text=Make%20getting%20in%20touch%20simple,that%20proves%20value%20and%20makes). The form should be labeled in the current language (and switch languages dynamically with the rest of the content).
- **Contact Details:** In addition to the form, list direct contact info for those who prefer it:
  - **Email address** (clickable mailto link).
  - **Phone number** (with international format if targeting an international audience, e.g. +32 for Belgium).
  - Perhaps a **LinkedIn profile link** or other professional social link (since agencies/startups often like checking LinkedIn for more background). These give options to the user - some might jump to send an email or call directly rather than filling a form.
- **Social Media (Optional):** If the freelancer has professional social media or a GitHub/ArtStation for showcasing work, icons can be placed here or in a footer. However, these should open in new tabs and not distract from contacting (the main goal at this stage). They mostly serve as extra proof of presence.
- **Footer Note (if any):** After the contact form, a small footer can include a language switcher again (as a secondary place to change language, which some multilingual sites do), copyright notice, or just a simple ©2026 and name. It's not a content-heavy element, so it can be minimal. The language switch duplication in the footer is not required but can be helpful for UX as some users scroll to bottom looking for language options[\[4\]](https://www.weglot.com/blog/website-language-selector#:~:text=Make%20It%20Easy%20to%20Find).
- **Relationship to User Goals:** This section is critical for the goal of **driving freelance inquiries**. It is designed to make reaching out as easy and straightforward as possible - which is why both a form and direct contact info are provided[\[7\]](https://www.wix.com/blog/best-portfolio-websites#:~:text=,links%20or%20a%20contact%20form)[\[3\]](https://www.wix.com/blog/best-portfolio-websites#:~:text=Make%20getting%20in%20touch%20simple,that%20proves%20value%20and%20makes). By now, the visitor has seen what they need; this section has a clear call-to-action (no ambiguity about what to do next - the user is explicitly invited to make contact). The wording is kept inviting and reassuring (e.g., offering a consultation, implying no obligation), to lower the barrier for contact[\[3\]](https://www.wix.com/blog/best-portfolio-websites#:~:text=Make%20getting%20in%20touch%20simple,that%20proves%20value%20and%20makes). The presence of multiple contact methods addresses different user preferences (some prefer email, others a quick form, etc.), again smoothing the path to inquiry. From a design/content perspective, nothing else should compete with this section's purpose - it's the climax of the page, so content here is focused purely on encouraging action (with the earlier sections having built up the interest and trust to do so). A successful contact section fulfilling these points will significantly support the conversion of visitors into actual freelance clients.

## Recommendations on Content Length & Toggle Features

- **Concise Default Content:** Throughout the site, default text will be kept concise (aiming for the key message in each section to be visible within a few lines) to give a clean and professional impression. For instance, each service section has a short intro paragraph and a few bullet points - enough to inform, but not so much as to deter a quick-reading business client. This recognizes that some visitors will skim, so the most important info (what service, what value) is front-loaded.
- **Expandable Details:** Where additional detail would benefit certain users (especially technical ones or those wanting proof), use **"Read more" toggles** or expandable sections:
- In the **Services** sections, hide the more technical lists or secondary details by default. For example, after listing the main tech stack in Web Development, an expandable text could reveal secondary technologies or elaborate on methodology (like testing, deployment process) for those interested. Similarly, in Real-Time Tech, an expansion could list specifics like "Networking in multiplayer via AWS GameLift" or other deep technical competencies that a tech-savvy client might appreciate. Casual visitors won't see this unless they choose to click, keeping the page from looking like a technical manual.
- In the **Portfolio** section, if project descriptions are lengthy, initially show just a teaser (project title and one line outcome). A visitor can click on the project to see a full case study or more images. This prevents the portfolio section from becoming text-heavy. Alternatively, each project card could expand on hover or click with a short paragraph of details, then collapse - an interactive way to let interested users dive deeper without leaving the page.
- For the **About Me** content, if the freelancer has a very extensive background, provide a snippet in the main view and allow a "Read more about my experience" expansion. This could reveal an extra paragraph or list of career highlights. This way the main page stays succinct, but those vetting the freelancer's full background (e.g., an agency wanting to ensure capability) can access more info[\[6\]](https://www.format.com/magazine/resources/photography/multiple-photography-niches#:~:text=If%20there%E2%80%99s%20a%20good%20reason,you%20are%20in%20your%20career).
- **When to Avoid Hiding Content:** Critical information that every visitor should see (the fact there are two services, the primary CTA, contact info) is never hidden behind toggles. We only hide secondary details or overly granular info to strike a balance. The rule of thumb: if removing the expanded content entirely would not impair the general understanding of the services and capabilities, then it's safe to keep it hidden by default.
- **User Experience with Toggles:** All "Read more" or toggle controls should be clearly indicated (e.g., a "+" icon or a link text that changes to "Read less" when expanded) and should function smoothly given the site will be built on a modern JS framework. Using a framework like Next.js on Vercel means these interactive elements can be implemented easily and without full page reloads, ensuring a slick user experience. Also, we'll ensure that expanding content doesn't accidentally mix the two service areas - each expansion is contained within its respective section.
- **Multilingual Toggle Behavior:** Each language version will have its content toggles operate in the same way, with the toggled text translated accordingly. It's important that, for example, the "Read more" link is translated ("Lire plus" in French, "Lees meer" in Dutch) and that expanding it shows the text in the correct language. This attention to detail maintains professionalism across locales.
- **Balancing Detail for Target Audience:** By using these expanding sections, we cater to both target sub-groups: the **business/client type** who wants high-level benefits and proof (they can read the headlines, see portfolio images, and be convinced), and the **technical/agency type** who might scan for specific technologies or want to know the process (they can click to see the deeper info). This approach ensures the content is **accessible and not overwhelming by default**, but **comprehensive** for those who seek more, aligning with the overall goal of being professional and appealing to various client types.

## Multilingual Content and Switching Guidance

- **Language Switch Visibility:** As noted, the language switcher is primarily in the header for easy discovery[\[4\]](https://www.weglot.com/blog/website-language-selector#:~:text=Make%20It%20Easy%20to%20Find). It should ideally be present on all pages/sections (since it's a single-page site, the header is fixed or repeated on top). Optionally, including a secondary language selector in the footer or as a floating element ensures that even if a user scrolls down without noticing the top, they have another chance to switch language at the bottom. The key is that it's **highly visible and labeled** (using language names or flags) so users recognize it instantly.
- **Default Language and URL Structure:** The site will default to French (e.g., the main URL showing French content). For SEO and clarity, consider using localized URLs or router paths for other languages (e.g., /en/ and /nl/ paths) if using Next.js or a similar framework. This way users can also share links directly to English or Dutch versions when needed. The content plan remains the same; it's just replicated in each language version.
- **Consistent Experience:** Maintain the same **structure and design across languages**. A visitor switching language should find themselves in the equivalent spot on the page in the new language. For instance, if an English user lands on the Real-Time section and toggles to French, the site should ideally stay on that section in French rather than bumping them to the top. This consistency is technically achievable with anchor tags or by preserving state, and it greatly improves UX for bilingual visitors.
- **Translation Tone:** Since French is primary, content will be crafted in French first, then translated. Ensure translations are **professional and culturally appropriate**. For example, some phrasing in marketing might differ in Dutch vs. English - use a native or professional translator to adapt the CTA and tagline nuances so they sound natural in each language. The tone across all should remain consistent (e.g., courteous, confident, not overly casual) to maintain a unified brand voice.
- **Navigation Labels:** Keep navigation labels short for all languages to fit nicely in the menu. (French and Dutch words can be longer; if needed, use one-word labels like "Web" and "VR/3D" in the nav for brevity, and the full "Web Development" written as a section header within the page).
- **Avoid Machine Translation Issues:** As content updates in one language, ensure a process to update others so no version lags behind. This content plan can guide translators on the intent of each section so the messaging stays consistent.
- **Testing:** Finally, as part of the plan, recommend testing the site in all three languages to ensure layout still looks good (e.g., some languages might wrap text differently or require smaller font to fit in buttons). The modern framework will help in delivering dynamic content per language without separate maintenance of multiple sites, which is ideal for efficiency.

## Keeping Both Services Visible but Distinct

- **Parallel Emphasis:** The layout should give **equal prominence** to Web Development and Real-Time Tech sections. For example, both could occupy roughly one full screen's height each (on desktop) initially, so that it's clear there are two main sections when scrolling. One shouldn't visually overpower the other. This signals that the freelancer is serious about both fields.
- **Visual Differentiation:** Use design elements to set the two sections apart. For instance, the Web Development section might have a different background color or illustration theme (e.g., graphics of code, websites, or abstract network imagery), while the Real-Time section could use another color or a theme imagery of 3D scenes or VR devices. This helps users subconsciously know when they've entered a new section focused on a different topic. Icons can also help - perhaps a **browser icon** or **code symbol** next to "Web Development" and a **3D cube or VR goggles icon** next to "Real-Time Tech" in titles. These visual cues reinforce the separation without needing extra explanation.
- **Navigation Aids:** In a single-page site, sticky navigation or a progress indicator can help keep context. For example, as the user scrolls, the corresponding menu item ("Web Development" or "Real-Time Tech") could highlight, so they always know which section they are in. This prevents any feeling of lost context, especially if the sections have different styling. If a user clicks on one of the service links in the header, the scroll should be smooth and perhaps offset such that the section title is clearly visible at top.
- **Cross-Links and References:** While each section stands alone, it might be useful occasionally to reference the other service briefly (since one person offers both). For example, at the end of the Web section's text, a subtle note: "_Need 3D or VR solutions? See the Real-Time Tech section below._" and vice versa. This can be a small hyperlink or callout. It gently reminds visitors that two distinct offerings exist and they can check the other if relevant, maintaining visibility of both services. This is especially useful if a visitor jumps directly to one section via a search or link - they might not realize the other exists without a hint.
- **Homepage Intro Covering Both:** As done in the hero and possibly in the About, ensure the top of the page (before splitting into sections) mentions both services together. This unified introduction is important so that visitors know the site is intentionally covering two areas. It's not necessary to go into detail in the intro, but a phrase like "_offering expertise in both web development and real-time 3D/VR technology_" sets the stage that the site will have two focal points. It manages expectations and legitimizes having two different sections.
- **Avoid Content Overlap:** Ensure that the content in each service section is specific to that service only. For example, avoid mentioning Unreal Engine or VR in the Web Dev description and vice versa (aside from maybe a one-line blurb in the about). Each section should read as if it's tailored to a client only interested in that domain. This clarity of focus in content will make each offering feel more specialized, which is important because clients want to feel you are an expert in **their** need area[\[8\]](https://www.format.com/magazine/resources/photography/multiple-photography-niches#:~:text=When%20photographers%20suggest%20that%20specializing,some%20professional%20and%20corporate%20headshots)[\[9\]](https://www.format.com/magazine/resources/photography/multiple-photography-niches#:~:text=Chances%20are%2C%20you%20answered%20the,of%20mouth%20in%20your%20industry) (clients often prefer specialists in their problem space). By siloing the content, you give that impression of specialization while still having both on one site.
- **Future Scaling - Possibly Separate Pages:** The content plan is optimized for a single-page site, but it's worth noting that if content grows too much (say numerous portfolio items or blog posts in each domain), the user can consider splitting into sub-pages in the future (one for Web, one for Real-Time) and have a common home page. The structure we've outlined would accommodate that: essentially each section here could become a full page later, with the home page offering the initial choice. For now, the single page is feasible and preferred; this plan keeps sections distinct enough that a split would be easy if needed down the line.

## Conclusion

This content plan provides a structured blueprint for a multilingual freelance portfolio website that effectively separates and highlights two distinct services. By organizing the site into clear sections (Hero, About, Web Development, Real-Time Tech, Portfolio, Contact) and carefully tailoring the content in each, the site will prevent confusion between the offerings while showcasing the freelancer's dual expertise. Every component has a defined purpose - from making a great first impression to providing social proof and encouraging contact - and each is aligned with the user's goals of professional presentation and lead generation. Important information is conveyed succinctly, with the option to delve deeper via "read more" expansions, ensuring the page remains clean and approachable to a broad audience (business clients, agencies, startups) without sacrificing detail for those who want it. Multilingual support is baked into the plan, with a prominent language switcher and content considerations for translation, so the site can welcome English and Dutch-speaking visitors as it grows.

In summary, the plan ensures **both services are visible, yet distinct**: a visitor can immediately understand the scope (thanks to the hero and navigation), explore the aspect they're interested in (web or real-time) with content speaking directly to their needs, see proof of capability (relevant projects), and easily reach out to initiate a collaboration. By following this content structure, the resulting website will be well-positioned to convert visitors into clients in 2026, effectively communicating the freelancer's unique blend of skills in a professional, user-friendly manner.
